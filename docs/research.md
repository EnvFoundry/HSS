# From a phenomenon to a research contribution

A research environment should connect the question, the material and the kind of claim being developed. The same phenomenon may support interpretive, descriptive, causal, predictive or normative work. A package name is selected only after this relationship becomes clear.

`hss explore brief.json` starts from the phenomenon, question, goal, scope and available materials. Its output identifies missing information and plausible routes. An Agent uses the selected guides and Skills to compare designs with the researcher. The conversation develops concepts, relevant scholarship, the unit and scope of study, alternative explanations or arguments, feasible material collection, the method's assumptions, and the intended contribution.

For an empirical project, translate the chosen design into named variables, timing, target quantities, observation units and computational tasks. Declare the comparison, assignment process or temporal structure that gives an estimate its meaning. Select the implementation that corresponds to those details. Several methods may address a question, and the same method may serve several questions; HSS therefore does not infer a causal design from a topic or a model name.

For interpretive and qualitative work, define the material, sampling logic, context, reading procedure and the relationship between passages and interpretation. Coding operations organize supplied annotations; they do not manufacture interviews or substitute word counts for an explanation. Historical work preserves the distinction between source production, transmission, textual differences and the historical argument. Normative and theoretical work develops concepts, premises, implications and competing formulations instead of forcing every argument into a regression task.

The executable study supplies computed results, tables, source passages or research instruments. The Agent interprets them within the chosen design, connects them to the literature and develops the manuscript. A completed paper requires a substantive contribution and actual source material; `results.md` is a results document, while the project manuscript file is a writing workspace.

Examples:

| Project | Starting point | Concrete output |
|---|---|---|
| Policy evaluation | Defined policy, comparison population, timing and outcome | Estimated effect using the selected design, tables, interpretation |
| Community experience | Research question and interview sampling | Anchored coding, interpreted themes, contrasting accounts |
| Historical textual study | Named textual witnesses and transmission context | Aligned variants, source interpretation, historical argument |
| Education measurement | Defined construct and response process | Pilot instrument, measurement model and interpretation |
| Philosophical analysis | Conceptual tension and relevant positions | Explicit premises, developed argument and implications |
| Literature synthesis | Bounded question and scholarly corpus | Structured synthesis or compatible-effect meta-analysis |

The seven workflows and eight Skills support these forms of work. Load only those relevant to the project. The researcher's decisions belong with the project brief and design, while shared method knowledge remains in HSS.
